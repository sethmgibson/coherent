# F01 — Whole-system simplification

Investigation procedure only. Catalog metadata lives in
[taxonomy.md](../taxonomy.md). Detection: semantic. Report in chat;
[shared boundaries](README.md) govern optional recording.

## Start from intended outcomes

Ask: **Given the outcomes this application must deliver, what is the smallest
complete system that could deliver them?**

**Infer from the codebase first.** Do not ask the user to describe the entire
application or enumerate every necessary outcome before investigating. Read
existing product and architecture guidance, inspect existing edits, and trace
representative user journeys from entrypoint through authoritative data,
decisions, side effects, and recovery. Use routes, UI actions, API contracts,
data models, permissions, tests, jobs, integrations, and relevant history to
infer who uses the application, what they need to accomplish, and what must be
true afterward. Follow the evidence needed for the proposal; an exhaustive
repository read is not required.

Present a concise inferred understanding with supporting evidence so the user
can correct it. Separate confirmed requirements from inferred intent. Source
and tests establish current behavior; they do not alone establish which
behavior the business still needs.

**Then ask only targeted business questions.** Ask about an unresolved business
requirement only when its answer could materially change the proposal and
available code and context cannot resolve it. Explain the uncertainty and how
the answer changes the design. For example: "These stages appear to move the
same records toward submission. Is separate approval at each stage a business
requirement, or can approval happen once at final submission?" Do not ask the
user to explain implementation details the repository can reveal. If no such
uncertainty remains, present the proposal without requiring a question round.

Do not assume existing features, screens, workflow stages, domain entities, or
service boundaries are necessary merely because they exist or have active
callers. Construct a substantially smaller alternative and test it against the
required outcomes. Look for changes that make whole families of code
unnecessary, and explain the causal chain of what disappears.

Start with a bounded overview and the strongest opportunity. Deepen that
proposal through conversation rather than reading every file or producing a
complete platform inventory. Missing business context makes the affected idea
conditional; continue investigating independent opportunities rather than
blocking discovery on a complete requirements interview. Do not treat an
unanswered question as confirmation. No questionnaire, CLI scan, initialization,
or tracking file is required.

## Six investigation lenses

Use these lenses together to construct a coherent alternative, rather than
returning a separate checklist of findings for each one. Apply them at the
useful scale: application, sub-application, workflow, page, component, or function.

1. **Rebuild from outcomes.** Sketch the smallest design that delivers the
   required outcomes without copying current navigation, modules, entities, or
   workflow stages. Compare it with the implementation. Try the strongest
   plausible reduction: could the primary experience become one page with a
   few actions? Identify exactly which requirements force complexity back in;
   fewer buttons alone do not establish a simpler system.
2. **Remove work created by the system itself.** Find workflows that reconcile
   competing representations, repair avoidable inconsistencies, transfer data
   between internal subsystems, or manage states introduced by an earlier
   design. Trace whether removing the cause would also remove reconciliation
   screens, repair jobs, settings, notifications, and supporting code.
3. **Collapse decisions and handoffs.** Examine review, selection, confirmation,
   export, import, and forwarding steps. Identify which require judgment,
   independent authority, or external coordination and which merely move
   information already available. Construct a workflow containing only the
   meaningful decisions and required boundaries.
4. **Unify journeys around the same outcome.** Investigate pages or
   sub-applications created independently that serve the same user outcome.
   Compare one workspace with contextual differences against separate flows.
   Trace roles, contracts, and failure cases; similar appearance or data shape
   does not establish equivalent behavior.
5. **Replace maintained information with derivation.** Find statuses, mappings,
   summaries, settings, and classifications people maintain even though
   authoritative inputs could determine them. Establish authority, freshness,
   historical meaning, and legitimate overrides before proposing derivation.
   Trace the forms, validation, synchronization, and recovery it would remove.
6. **Challenge the platform's scope.** Consider whether an existing provider,
   existing capability, or simpler operating process could absorb an entire
   subsystem. Include replacement limitations, ongoing cost, dependencies,
   access boundaries, and manual work. Count complexity moved to operators or
   another service when comparing the complete system.

## Test the proposal against reality

Distinguish these conclusions in ordinary language:

- **Unnecessary behavior:** evidence supports removing it while preserving the
  required outcomes.
- **A simpler design:** the outcome remains necessary, but a different workflow
  could deliver it. Explain equivalence and what still needs validation.
- **A product or policy decision:** the reduction depends on changing a
  requirement, supported use case, or business rule. Present it as a conditional
  idea, not an established defect or an authorized deletion.

Seek the strongest counterexample: another role, an infrequent task, an external
consumer, a failure, or a recovery scenario. Preserve required authorization,
audit history, accessibility, financial semantics, uncertainty, and recovery.
A one-page experience may still need substantial underlying behavior. Missing
usage evidence is not proof of no use; moving work off-screen or into manual
operations is not automatically simplification.

Be ambitious about alternatives and explicit about assumptions. Do not claim
the platform can collapse based only on a sample of its workflows. State the
examined scope and unexamined areas. A justified existing boundary or no
worthwhile reduction is a successful result.

F01 concerns whether behavior and whole journeys are needed. A01 concerns
architecture that no longer matches living workflows; B01 concerns added
parallel structure; C05 concerns how a necessary workflow is distributed.
Use those classes for implementation findings without forcing an F01 review.

## Explain the strongest opportunity in chat

Lead with the concrete reduction, then explain enough to assess it:

- Current workflow → smaller alternative → what disappears.
- Required outcomes and boundaries that remain, with source locations and
  relevant callers, contracts, tests, or other evidence.
- Conclusion type, confidence, unresolved assumptions, and the counterexample
  most likely to invalidate it.
- Practical benefit, implementation risk, and the smallest useful slice that
  could validate the design. Label estimates; do not invent savings.

For example: "One authoritative operation could replace three maintained
stages, removing two screens and their synchronization job. Final independent
approval remains. This depends on whether intermediate stages carry any
separate business authority."

Let the conversation refine the idea: "go more radical," "show what disappears,"
"challenge that assumption," "keep this capability," or "trace recovery cases."
Use new answers to revise the proposal rather than restarting the investigation.
After findings, offer to investigate further, implement a concrete proposal, or
move on to architecture fossilization. Do not require every question to be
resolved before reviewing another class.

An audit does not authorize application edits or changes to business policy.
An implementation request authorizes the selected proposal within its agreed
constraints; follow [fix-safety.md](../fix-safety.md) without another routine
approval round. Check required outcomes and failure/recovery behavior, then
inspect code made obsolete by the change. Explain smaller cleanup findings
that the proposal would supersede before investing in them. F01 is independently
selectable and adds no catalog prerequisites to other rules; a particular fix
may have a genuine dependency established by evidence.

## Advanced recording (explicit opt-in only)

Use the existing semantic `Finding` shape in `decisions.json` only when
persistent review is requested. Use `ruleId: F01`, `detectionMode: semantic`,
and a stable `identity` such as `whole-system:<outcome-or-capability>` without
line numbers. Include exact locations, required outcomes and equivalence
evidence, `authoritativeConcept` when established, `deletionOpportunity`,
`cleanupBenefit`, `changeRisk`, and `testSafetyEvidence`.

Record a source-backed opportunity as a candidate until reviewed. A conditional
product or policy idea stays in chat unless the user selects it for tracking;
keep unresolved requirements explicit in its evidence and defer it with
`missingEvidence` or `reconsiderWhen` rather than marking it ready. Do not add
blanket prerequisites or invent a mechanical detector result. F01 has no detector.
